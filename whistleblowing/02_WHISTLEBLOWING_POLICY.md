## 1. Purpose

Data & More ApS wants people to speak up about wrongdoing without fear. This policy explains:

- what you can report;
- how to report it, including to the independent National Whistleblower Scheme;
- how Data & More will handle a report;
- the protection you have against retaliation.

It applies alongside, and does not replace, your right to report to the National Whistleblower Scheme or to a competent authority.

## 2. Scope

This policy covers **work-related concerns** involving Data & More ApS or people acting on its behalf, raised by:

- employees;
- self-employed contractors;
- shareholders and members of management or the board;
- volunteers;
- paid and unpaid interns;
- people working under contractors, subcontractors or suppliers;
- former employees;
- job applicants and people in pre-contractual negotiations.

## 3. What you can report

You can report:

1. **Breaches of EU law** within the fields covered by Directive (EU) 2019/1937 — including public procurement, financial services, anti-money laundering, product safety, transport safety, environmental protection, food and feed safety, public health, consumer protection, privacy and data protection, and network and information security.
2. **Serious criminal offences and other serious matters** under Danish law — for example bribery, fraud, theft, embezzlement, wiretapping, harassment, discrimination, serious safety breaches, or attempts to conceal such matters.

You do not need proof. You need **reasonable grounds** to believe the information is true and within scope at the time you report.

## 4. What is not covered

This is not a general grievance or complaints channel. It is not the right route for:

- ordinary dissatisfaction with pay, workload, rota or management style;
- personal conflicts between colleagues;
- customer complaints about a product or service;
- a matter you learned about only as a private citizen, unrelated to work.

Those should go through the normal HR, management or customer-complaint route. If you are unsure, ask the Speak-Up Contact — asking a question is not a report and does not commit you to anything.

The Act also excludes certain categories, including classified information, lawyer–client privilege, health professionals' confidentiality, court deliberations and criminal procedure. If your concern touches these areas, seek advice before reporting.

## 5. How to report

### 5.1 Primary route — National Whistleblower Scheme

You can report directly to **Den Nationale Whistleblowerordning**:

- **Web:** [whistleblower.dk](https://whistleblower.dk)
- **Phone:** 33 19 32 02
- **Email:** wb@datatilsynet.dk
- **In person:** by arrangement with the scheme

The scheme is run by **Datatilsynet** and is independent of Data & More. You can report in writing, by phone or in person. **Anonymous reporting is possible.**

You do **not** need Data & More's permission, and you do **not** have to report internally first.

### 5.2 Optional internal route — Speak-Up Contact

If you would prefer to talk to someone at Data & More first, contact:

- **Speak-Up Contact:** [Compliance Lead name], [email], [phone]
- **Backup:** [Backup contact name], [email], [phone]

The Speak-Up Contact will:

- listen and explain your options;
- help you use `whistleblower.dk` if you choose that route;
- record the concern if you ask Data & More to handle it internally;
- keep your identity confidential as far as the law allows.

The Speak-Up Contact will **not** investigate the matter personally. If the concern involves the Speak-Up Contact, management or the board, it must be routed to [Chair / Board contact].

### 5.3 What to include

If you can, tell us:

- what happened, and when;
- where it happened;
- who was involved;
- whether anyone else knows;
- whether there is documentary evidence, and where it is;
- whether you have already reported it elsewhere.

Do not investigate the matter yourself, and do not access information you are not authorised to see. Preserve evidence; do not delete or alter it.

## 6. Protection against retaliation

Data & More prohibits retaliation against anyone who reports in good faith, including:

- dismissal, demotion, reassignment or reduced duties;
- withholding pay, benefits or training;
- exclusion, ostracism or gossip;
- a negative performance review or reference;
- threats, intimidation or obstruction;
- any other detriment connected to the report.

This protection also covers:

- someone who confidentially helps you report (a facilitator);
- third parties connected to you who may suffer retaliation;
- companies or authorities you own or work for.

If you believe you have suffered retaliation, tell the Speak-Up Contact or the National Scheme immediately. If the concern involves the Speak-Up Contact, tell the Board contact. Data & More will investigate. In any later dispute, if you show that you made a report and suffered a detriment, **Data & More must prove that the detriment was not retaliation** (§29).

Reporting in good faith is protected even if the concern is not confirmed. Knowingly making a false report is not protected and may be a disciplinary matter.

## 7. How Data & More will handle a report

Where Data & More receives a report directly, it will:

1. **Acknowledge** receipt within **7 calendar days**.
2. **Assess** the concern and check for conflicts within **5 working days**.
3. **Investigate** proportionately, using an impartial person. The investigation may be handled internally, by an external adviser, or by the National Scheme.
4. **Give feedback** on the outcome within **3 months** of the acknowledgment, or explain why more time is needed.
5. **Check for retaliation** at 30, 90 and 180 days after the report.
6. **Record** the case in the case register and keep the file access-restricted.
7. **Retain** the file only as long as necessary and then delete it under the retention rule.

Where the National Scheme handles the report, Data & More will cooperate fully and respond to requests for information.

## 8. Confidentiality

Data & More will protect the identity of the reporter, the person accused and any third party named in the report. Identity will be shared only with people who need it to handle the case, unless:

- the reporter gives explicit consent;
- disclosure is required by law;
- disclosure is necessary to investigate the matter or to protect someone's legal rights.

Data & More will tell the reporter before any disclosure, unless doing so would jeopardise the investigation.

## 9. Data protection

- Reports are processed only as necessary to handle the concern.
- Access is restricted to the Speak-Up Contact, the investigation team and management where needed.
- Special-category data is avoided unless it is necessary for the case.
- The case register contains the minimum data needed to track the case.
- Case files are stored in a restricted folder, not in a personal mailbox or shared drive.
- Retention follows the rule in the procedure; data is deleted when no longer needed.
- A data protection impact assessment screening will be completed before launch, and reviewed if the scheme changes.

## 10. Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Management | Approves the policy; ensures resources; receives aggregated metrics; does not interfere in individual cases |
| Compliance Lead | Owns the scheme; acts as Speak-Up Contact; maintains the register; reports metrics; runs the annual review |
| Backup contact | Covers absence and conflicts |
| Managers | Recognise a report, do not investigate alone, escalate promptly, do not retaliate |
| All staff | Report concerns in good faith; cooperate with investigations; do not obstruct |

## 11. Review

This policy is reviewed annually, and earlier if:

- headcount reaches 45 or 50;
- a customer contract or sector order requires a statutory scheme;
- the Act is amended;
- a serious case reveals a gap;
- the scheme is outsourced or materially changed.
