# Whistleblowing Procedure

**Data & More ApS**
**Version:** 1.0
**Effective date:** [Approval date]
**Owner:** Compliance Lead
**Review:** Annually

---

## 1. Purpose and scope

This procedure explains how Data & More handles a whistleblowing report. It applies to every report received by a manager, the Speak-Up Contact or management, whether it arrived in person, by phone, by email, by letter or through another channel.

It does not apply to reports handled solely by the National Whistleblower Scheme, except for the cooperation duty in section 12 of this procedure.

## 2. Roles

| Role | Who | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Speak-Up Contact | [Compliance Lead] | First point of contact; acknowledgment; conflict check; case coordination; register |
| Backup | [Backup contact] | Covers absence, holiday or conflict |
| Case handler | Appointed per case | Conducts the investigation; may be internal or external |
| Decision-maker | Management, or the Board for concerns involving management | Approves the outcome and any action |
| Board contact | [Chair] | Handles concerns involving the Speak-Up Contact, management or the board |
| National Scheme | Datatilsynet | External reporting route; may investigate directly |

**Separation of roles:** the person who receives the report may coordinate the case but should not be the sole investigator, decision-maker and record-keeper. For a small company, the minimum is: one person coordinates, a second person reviews the outcome.

## 3. Routes into the procedure

| Route | What happens |
|---|---|
| Reporter uses `whistleblower.dk` | The National Scheme handles it. Data & More cooperates if contacted. No internal case file unless Data & More receives a request |
| Reporter speaks to a manager | Manager listens, does not investigate, explains the routes, and informs the Speak-Up Contact the same day |
| Reporter speaks to the Speak-Up Contact | Speak-Up Contact follows section 4 |
| Anonymous report received internally | Record it; assess whether it can be investigated without identifying the reporter; do not attempt to identify them |
| Report concerns the Speak-Up Contact, management or the board | Route directly to the Board contact; do not discuss with the subject |

## 4. Receiving a report

### 4.1 First conversation

The receiver should:

1. Thank the person and reassure them that reporting is protected.
2. Explain the two routes: `whistleblower.dk` (independent) or internal handling by Data & More.
3. Ask what outcome they want: to report externally, to have Data & More handle it, or to talk it through before deciding.
4. Explain that Data & More cannot promise absolute confidentiality, but will share identity only on a need-to-know basis.
5. Ask for the facts, in the reporter's own words. Do not cross-examine, speculate or promise outcomes.
6. Ask whether there is evidence and where it is. Advise them not to investigate or delete anything.
7. Explain the next steps and the 7-day acknowledgment.
8. Record the conversation using the oral-report minute template if the report is oral.

### 4.2 What not to do

- Do not tell the person they are wrong or that the matter is not serious.
- Do not promise that no one else will find out.
- Do not contact the accused, witnesses or colleagues before an assessment.
- Do not investigate alone if the concern involves you or someone close to you.
- Do not delete, alter or move evidence.
- Do not discuss the case in open areas or over unsecured channels.

### 4.3 Same-day escalation

The receiver must inform the Speak-Up Contact the same day, unless the Speak-Up Contact is the subject. If the Speak-Up Contact is the subject, inform the Board contact.

## 5. Initial assessment

Within **5 working days** of receipt, the Speak-Up Contact will:

1. Open a case file and assign a case ID.
2. Check for conflicts — personal, reporting-line, financial or other.
3. Confirm the concern is within scope. If it is not, record why and tell the reporter, where possible.
4. Assess urgency: risk to people, ongoing harm, data breach, financial loss, evidence loss, regulatory notification.
5. Decide the investigation route: internal, external adviser, or referral to the National Scheme, police or regulator.
6. Record the decision and the reason.

If the matter may be a personal data breach, notify the DPO/Privacy Lead immediately (or the Compliance Lead if no DPO is appointed) and follow the GDPR breach procedure. If a crime may be in progress, consider the police.

## 6. Acknowledgment

Send the acknowledgment within **7 calendar days** of receipt. Use Template A in `06_TEMPLATES.md`.

The acknowledgment must:

- confirm receipt;
- give the case ID;
- explain the next steps and the expected timeline;
- name the contact point;
- remind the reporter of the non-retaliation protection;
- explain how to report a concern about the handling itself.

If the reporter is anonymous and cannot be contacted, record that acknowledgment was not possible.

## 7. Investigation

### 7.1 Terms of reference

Before starting, the case handler records:

- the concern, in one paragraph;
- the scope and any exclusions;
- the questions to answer;
- the evidence to review;
- the people to interview;
- the target date;
- the confidentiality and conflict arrangements.

### 7.2 Principles

- **Proportionate:** match the effort to the seriousness and complexity.
- **Impartial:** the investigator has no conflict and is not the subject.
- **Fair:** the accused person is told the substance of the allegation before any adverse finding, and has a chance to respond.
- **Documented:** interviews are minuted; documents are indexed; decisions are reasoned.
- **Confidential:** need-to-know only.
- **Evidence-preserving:** secure documents, emails, logs and devices; do not alter metadata.

### 7.3 Interviews

- Interview the reporter first, then witnesses, then the accused.
- Use the oral-report minute or an interview note.
- Give the interviewee the chance to correct the note.
- Do not promise confidentiality to witnesses beyond what can be delivered.
- Keep the accused's identity confidential until they need to know.

### 7.4 External support

Use an external adviser where:

- the matter is complex or high-risk;
- the accused is senior;
- the internal team lacks the expertise;
- the reporter asks for external handling;
- the National Scheme refers the matter back with an expectation of independent investigation.

## 8. Decision and outcome

The decision-maker records one of:

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Substantiated** | The concern is confirmed on the balance of probabilities |
| **Not substantiated** | The evidence does not support the concern |
| **Inconclusive** | The evidence is insufficient to decide |
| **Referred** | The matter has been referred to the National Scheme, police, regulator or another process |
| **Withdrawn** | The reporter has withdrawn; the case may still be reviewed for systemic issues |

The decision record includes:

- the findings;
- the evidence relied on;
- the reasons;
- any disciplinary, contractual, control or policy action;
- any referral;
- any systemic learning.

## 9. Feedback

Send feedback within **3 months** of the acknowledgment. Use Template B in `06_TEMPLATES.md`.

The feedback should tell the reporter:

- that the matter has been looked into;
- the outcome at a level that does not breach confidentiality or prejudice an investigation;
- what action has been taken, in general terms;
- what happens next;
- how to raise a concern about the handling;
- that the non-retaliation protection continues.

If more time is needed, tell the reporter before the 3-month point and give a revised date. Record the reason.

If the reporter is anonymous and cannot be contacted, record that feedback was not possible.

## 10. Non-retaliation monitoring

At **30, 90 and 180 days** after the report, the Speak-Up Contact checks whether the reporter has suffered any detriment:

- change in duties, shifts, pay or benefits;
- performance review or disciplinary action;
- exclusion from meetings, projects or training;
- changed reporting line;
- reference or job security;
- informal pressure or gossip.

Record the check. If a detriment is alleged, open a separate review and inform the decision-maker. Do not ask the reporter to prove retaliation; document the facts and the reasons for the decision.

## 11. Records and retention

| Record | Location | Access | Retention |
|---|---|---|---|
| Case register | Restricted folder / register file | Speak-Up Contact, backup, decision-maker | [Retention period] after closure |
| Case file | Restricted case folder | Case handler, Speak-Up Contact, decision-maker | [Retention period] after closure |
| Oral report minute | Case file | As above | As above |
| Acknowledgment and feedback | Case file | As above | As above |
| Retaliation checks | Case file | As above | As above |
| Annual metrics | Compliance folder | Management, auditor | 3 years |

**Retention rule:** keep records only as long as necessary and proportionate. Proposed default: 24 months after closure, then delete unless a legal hold, investigation, insurance or litigation obligation requires longer. Confirm the period with a legal adviser and record the deletion.

**Access:** role-based, least privilege. No personal mailboxes. No shared drives open to all staff. Log access where the platform supports it.

**Case file index:** open Template G in `06_TEMPLATES.md` at the start of every case and update it as documents are added.

## 12. Cooperation with the National Scheme

If the National Scheme contacts Data & More:

- nominate one point of contact (the Speak-Up Contact);
- respond within the deadline given;
- provide only the information requested;
- do not contact the reporter unless the scheme asks you to;
- do not retaliate or take pre-emptive action against the reporter;
- record the request and the response in the case file;
- tell the scheme if you cannot comply and why.

## 13. Interface with other processes

| Process | Interface |
|---|---|
| GDPR breach | If the report reveals a personal data breach, follow the breach procedure and notify the DPO/Privacy Lead immediately (or the Compliance Lead if no DPO is appointed) |
| Security incident | If the report reveals a security incident, open an incident record; keep the whistleblowing file separate |
| HR disciplinary | The investigation may feed a disciplinary process; the whistleblowing file stays separate from the personnel file |
| Criminal matter | Refer to the police where appropriate; preserve evidence |
| Regulatory matter | Refer to the competent authority; record the referral |
| Insurance / legal | Notify the adviser or insurer if a claim is threatened |

## 14. Annual review

The Compliance Lead reviews the scheme annually using `09_ANNUAL_REVIEW_CHECKLIST.md` and reports metrics to management. The review covers:

- case volume and routes;
- timeliness (7-day acknowledgment, 3-month feedback);
- outcomes;
- retaliation allegations;
- training completion;
- register quality;
- retention and deletion;
- legal changes and trigger points.
